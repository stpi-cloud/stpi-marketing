## No single board sees the whole market _(1 day before fetch)_

![card](bible-assets/whop/ebfaad1de2c2138f95fbc7ed.png)

Every employer in the region spreads listings across a mix of national portals, niche sites and their own career pages. A search that stops at one source is a search that stops at a slice.

**Board-specific habits** — Large boards tend to post high-volume, entry-level roles on a daily cadence, while niche boards focus on specialised functions and update less frequently. Company pages often appear only when a vacancy is urgent. Recognising these patterns lets you allocate time where it matters.

**Duplicate postings as a signal** — When the same role appears on three or more boards, the market is telling you the position is real and time-sensitive. Treat duplicates as high-priority items, not as wasted effort.

**Scheduling the sweep** — The biggest operational leak is checking the same boards at random times. Build a repeatable schedule: morning run on the two national portals, mid-day scan of the top niche sites, afternoon check of company pages. Consistency turns a chaotic search into a predictable workflow.

**Quality control for clients** — Keep a log of each board visited, time spent and any duplicates flagged. Share that log with clients as proof of diligence; it turns hours into transparent value.

**Brand impact** — Delivering a full-market snapshot reinforces your practice's reputation for thoroughness. Clients notice the difference between a "we looked everywhere" report and a list that only reflects one board's feed.

Treat board coverage as a scheduling problem, not a data-collection problem. Align your daily routine with board habits, flag duplicates, log the process, and let the consistency speak for your brand.

## The caps are the feature _(Sep 23)_

Most job hunt tools encourage high volume, treating applications as a numbers game. STPi operates differently. We build guardrails into the system, not bottlenecks, to prevent burnout and encourage focused effort.

Our approval gate, for instance, requires a human click before anything sends. This simple step prevents reactive submissions and ensures every application is deliberate. The 30-day cooldown on reapplying to the same role or employer serves a similar purpose, preventing over-eagerness and promoting strategic engagement.

These are not limitations; they are design choices. The goal is to make burnout unsendable, to enforce a sustainable pace. The free demo reflects this, allowing 25 drafts and 5 sends per rolling 30 days. This core philosophy applies across all product tiers.

STPi focuses on quality over unchecked quantity, helping users manage the process rather than being overwhelmed by it.

## We put a human approval gate inside a job application robot, on purpose _(Sep 20)_

Most job-hunt tools optimize the same thing: send more, faster, with AI-polished wording everywhere. We built the opposite trade on purpose and it is worth explaining why, because the reasoning matters even if you never touch our product.

A search has two very different halves. The boring eighty percent (reading listings, scoring fit against what a job description actually repeats, tailoring drafts, timing follow-ups, keeping a tracker honest) is language logistics, and it is fully automatable. The twenty percent that carries your name, your judgment, your voice in the room, is not, and the moment a tool sends that part unattended it stops being leverage and starts being liability.

So the machine we shipped, stpi, runs the first half on a schedule and hard-gates the second: every application sits behind an approval click, sends are spaced, daily drafts are capped, and a 30-day cooldown per employer is enforced in code, not policy. Burnout volume is not discouraged; it is physically unavailable. The caps run the same way in the free demo (25 drafts, 5 sends, 30 days) as in the paid tiers, which start at thirty dollars because support costs money.

The open questions I would genuinely like this forum's opinion on: what should an approval gate refuse by default? Which two columns of an application tracker would you never give up? If the answers get good, they ship.
