# STPi — the Emotion Engine (AFGR selling system)

**Status:** canonical selling layer, added 2026-10-14. Sits *on top of* `pitch.md`,
`sales-scripts.md`, `pricing.md`, `faq-objections.md` — it does not replace them.
Those files carry the facts. This file carries the **order, voltage and timing**
that make the facts move a buyer.

**House rules still govern every line below** (BRAND.md): plain words, specific
beats impressive, the human is the hero, never "bot", never "guaranteed
interviews", never invent a number. Emotion here means *revealing a true cost
more vividly* — never fabricating one. If a line needs a false claim to work,
the line is wrong, not the prospect.

---

## 1 · Why facts alone don't close STPi deals

A coach can agree with every fact we have — 17 boards, one tap, 72-hour purge,
$0 hosting — and still do nothing. Agreement is logical. Buying is emotional.
The formula for every STPi conversation:

```
SALE = (EMOTION × LOGICAL PERMISSION) ÷ FRICTION
```

- **Emotion** = why act, why now.
- **Logical permission** = the numbers the buyer repeats to a partner, a
  principal, a spouse, or themselves at 02:00. (`pricing.md` lines, verbatim.)
- **Friction** = setup effort, AI distrust, "will it invent things?", "will my
  mailbox get flagged?" (`faq-objections.md` exists to delete friction.)

STPi starts every deal with a **double fear problem**: the prospect fears the
06:00 queue (inaction) *and* fears an AI touching their candidates (action).
Every call must grow the first fear and shrink the second:

```
FEAR OF DOING NOTHING  must beat  FEAR OF THE MACHINE
(missed ads, capped bench,        (invention, spam flags,
Sunday typing, candidates lost     loss of control,
to faster applicants)              embarrassment in front of clients)
```

The tap is our fear-of-action killer. Lead with it, demo it, close on it.

## 2 · The sequence: ANGER → FEAR → GREED → RELIEF

Never open with greed ("make more money!"). Never close on fear (it freezes).
The order below is mechanical, not decorative.

| Stage | Job | In STPi language it sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| **ANGER** — open | Break indifference. Name the villain. | "Your candidates aren't losing to better candidates. They're losing to faster ones — the ads are posted at 06:00, there are hundreds of applications in by lunch, and yours are still being typed." |
| **FEAR** — urgency | Make waiting the risky option. | "Every candidate you take on adds Sunday typing. So your income is capped by your typing speed. If nothing changes, where is that in 12 months?" |
| **GREED** — desire | Make the win personal and possessed. | "What would you do with those hours back — take on two more candidates, or take Fridays back? One placement fee covers months of this." |
| **RELIEF** — close | Make acting feel safe and small. | "Nothing sends without your tap. Setup is ten minutes, we stay on the call. The demo is free forever; you upgrade when your second candidate asks." |

**The enemy is never the prospect and never us.** The enemy is: the 06:00
queue, the 40 open tabs, speed beating quality, portal deadlines, ghostwritten
11pm letters, and the admin that eats billable hours. Stand shoulder-to-shoulder
against it.

## 3 · Diagnose the buyer in 3 minutes

| Archetype | Spot them by | Lead emotion | Greed currency |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Solo coach at capacity** | "It's just me." Sighs about evenings/Sundays. | Anger at typing → Relief | Time back, bench growth without an assistant |
| **CV/job-search studio** | Talks bundles, margins, throughput. | Greed | More bundles/week at the same price, their brand on the PDF |
| **Boutique agency / recruiter** | Talks placements, clients, candidate-side vs ATS. | Fear + Greed | Candidate-side desk, audit trail for client reporting |
| **Career centre / TVET / NGO** | Talks cohorts, officers, reports, funders. | Fear (invisible graduates) → Relief | Queue depth per officer, donor-ready numbers, data stays home (Sovereign) |

One emotion dominates. Sell to that one; use the other two as pressure.

## 4 · The question bank (their words become your close)

Ask. Shut up. Write answers down **verbatim** — you will quote them at the close.

**Anger (find the villain)**
1. "Walk me through a candidate's week with you — boards, letters, sends. Which part would you never bill a client for?"
2. "Tell me about the last role your candidate should have landed. What actually happened?"
3. "When an employer gets hundreds of applications by lunch, where does a hand-typed one land in that queue?"
4. "What are you doing on Sundays that a machine should be doing?"

**Fear (price the inaction, in their numbers)**
5. "How many hours a week is hunt-and-rewrite, honestly? And what's an hour of your coaching worth?"
6. "What happens when you're fully booked — which candidates get the thin version of you?"
7. "If nothing changes for 12 months: better, same, or worse? Why?"
8. "When a candidate quietly stops paying you, what reason do they usually give themselves?"

**Greed (build the possessed future)**
9. "If the hunt-and-rewrite vanished tomorrow, what would you do with those hours first?"
10. "How many candidates are sitting on your bench right now without a pipeline? What is one placement — or one bundle — worth to you?"
11. "Are you trying to grow the bench, get your evenings back, or both?"

**Relief / permission (delete friction early)**
12. "If you tried this, what would need to be true for you to feel safe pressing approve the first time?"
13. "Who else would want a say before you paid for it?" *(Surface the partner/principal now. Hand them `one-pager-partner.md`.)*

## 5 · The 20-minute AFGR call

```
0–3   ANGER   Show their Tuesday back to them. Name the 06:00 queue. Get THEM
              to say the typing is the bottleneck (Q1–Q4).
3–7   FEAR    Build THEIR cost number: hours/week × their rate × 4.3 weeks.
              Then Q7. Let silence work. ("Worse" is the only honest answer.)
7–11  GREED   One placement / bundle value vs $306. Bench waiting (Q10).
              Future-pace one scene: "It's 06:10. The ad landed at 06:00.
              Your candidate's draft is already waiting for your tap."
11–17 DEMO    Three scenes only (see §6). No feature tour.
17–20 CLOSE   Relief stack: tap control → 10-minute setup → free demo →
              price vs one placement. Recap close in their words. Then SILENCE.
```

**Recap close (use their verbatim answers):**
> "You said [anger, their words]. You said twelve months of the same means
> [fear, their words]. And the first thing you'd do with the hours back is
> [greed, their words]. The demo gives you that on one candidate, free
> forever, and nothing sends without your tap. Shall we set it up now while
> I'm on the line — first card lands by the next hunt slot?"

## 6 · The demo is three emotional scenes, not a tour

1. **REVENGE (anger paid off, ~2 min).** Open the approval surface (dashboard;
   Discord/Telegram where that's the tenant's surface). Show one card for a
   real-feeling match (demo persona only — never a real candidate). Read the
   match reason slowly. Open the letter: second paragraph, company named, two
   real requirements mirrored. "This is the letter that used to be typed at 23:00."
2. **NIGHTMARE AVOIDED (fear paid off, ~2 min).** Show coverage: boards swept,
   the ~85% thrown out free by rules + pre-score, the send caps, the 72-hour
   purge. "This is what runs at 06:00 while you're asleep — and this is
   everything it refused to waste your attention on."
3. **JACKPOT (greed paid off, ~3 min).** Their numbers, live: hours back per
   month, bench candidates now coverable, one placement/bundle vs the tier
   price. Then the tap: approve one, reject one, watch it delete. Invite them
   to check the letter against the CV line by line — *"If it invented one
   fact, delete the demo and tell everyone."* A prospect who tried to catch
   the machine inventing, and failed, has no fear-of-action left.

## 7 · Follow-up is emotional rotation, not "checking in"

One emotion per touch. Stop on any reply. Full scripts: `sales-scripts-emotion.md §7`,
outreach sequences: `cold-outreach-emotion-rotation.md`.

| When | Emotion | Beat |
|---|---|---|
| Same day | Relief | "Setup slot held. Ten minutes, I stay on the call. First card by the next hunt." |
| Day 2 | Fear | Their cost number, restated: "That's ±R[x]/month of typing at your rate." |
| Day 4 | Greed | Bench maths: waiting candidates × one placement/bundle value. |
| Day 7 (post-demo) | Pride/Greed | Their real counts: cards, taps, sends. "Your bench called." (Solo nudge.) |
| Day 10 | Anger | The enemy content: the 06:00 queue, speed beating quality. No ask. |
| Day 14 | Loss/closure | "Closing the file — keep the free demo either way. One word ('later') and I return next quarter." |

## 8 · Red lines (non-negotiable)

1. No invented competitor adoption, deadlines, slots or results. STPI's market
   is small; coaches talk to coaches. One exposed bluff ends the vertical.
2. Their numbers, cut by them. If a deal only closes on inflated maths, it
   closes into a refund.
3. Anger at the queue, the tabs, the typing — never shame at the coach.
4. "Guaranteed interviews" is banned language (BRAND.md). We sell coverage,
   hours and judgement at scale. The tap is the trust.
5. The 02:00 test: comfortable with their partner/principal hearing the call
   recording? If not, rewrite the line.
