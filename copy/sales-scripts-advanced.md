# Sales scripts — advanced set (2026-10-01)

Second volume. Same rules as the first: the human is the hero, nothing sends
without a tap, the demo is free forever, the 7-day hosted trial is called
exactly that, one CTA per artifact, USD prices with the rand figure as "about".
Every script ends in a question or a demo — never in applause.

## A · University career centre (departmental, budget season)

**Opener (in person or call).**
"Your office sees hundreds of CVs a year and maybe twelve get tailored per role.
That's not a quality problem; it's an hours problem. I run a pipeline that reads
the boards for you and drafts the tailoring pass overnight — your advisors keep
the judgment, the machine does the reading. Can I show you one approval card?"

**The honesty beat (use early, it disarms procurement).**
"Two things it will never do: send anything without one of your advisors tapping
approve, and keep any CV longer than the decision needs. If those two are deal
breakers in the other direction, tell me now and save us both a month."

**The pilot framing.**
Solo is deliberately the size of one desk: 1 advisor, up to 3 candidates, one
term. If one graduate placement this term traces to a 06:00 hunt nobody staffed,
you have the business case for the institutional conversation — and that
conversation is Sovereign, not "more Solos".

**RFP line that gets repeated.**
"Data stays with you: Solo on our hosted rails keeps documents for at most
72 hours; Sovereign keeps everything on your own servers with a signed
licence. Your counsel will ask exactly one question about data flow, and this
is the one-sentence answer."

## B · Outplacement firm (corporate RFP)

**The door.** "When you offboard fifty people, twelve get your A-team. What do
the other thirty-eight get — a PDF and good luck?"

**The offer.** STPi gives every affected employee their own hunt: boards swept
on a schedule, fit scored against their real CV, tailored drafts behind the
employee's own approval tap. Your firm reports hunt activity per cohort —
the number your client's CFO actually wanted in the outplacement budget line.

**The differentiator statement.** "We are not a CV library and not a motivational
webinar. We are the machine that makes thirty-eight people visible to the market
on week one instead of month three."

**Close.** "Send me your next cohort size and country split; I'll send back a
one-page coverage plan priced per seat — and one free demo seat you can run on
your own machine this week to check my arithmetic."

## C · NGO / workforce-development programme

**The door.** "Your funding report says 'beneficiaries placed'. Your team says
'we can't read two hundred job ads a day'. Both are true; the gap between them
is queue depth, and queue depth is what I sell."

**Funding-frame.** Studio at about R9,999 a month runs fifteen beneficiaries with
the NGO's letterhead on every document. For donor reports: hunt volume, sends
per beneficiary, interview flags — the pipeline produces the metrics the
programme officer currently types by hand.

**The trust paragraph.** "Consent is built in, not announced: every beneficiary
(or their case worker) taps approve before anything leaves, drafts purge in
72 hours, and we never store a database of your beneficiaries on our side —
Sovereign exists precisely for programmes whose funders ask."

## D · TVET college /skills centre (ZA-ZW-ZM corridor)

**The door.** "Your graduates are skilled and invisible. The adverts that fit
them are on PNet, VacancyMail and iHarareJobs at 06:00; your placement officer
sees them at 14:00, behind three hundred others."

**The wedge.** "$306 a month, about R4,999, runs three graduates through the same
wheel paying agencies use. If one graduate signs in the first term, the college
recovered the year cost in a single placement story — and you can quote the
hunt log in your newsletter home."

**Objection: 'our learners don't have laptops'.** The operator does — one
placement officer with a laptop and a WhatsApp group runs the whole cohort's
queue from one Discord. Candidates never need Discord themselves: the intake
link is a form, and approval taps belong to the officer.

## E · HR consultancy moving into career services

**The door.** "You already write the CVs. What you don't have is the hunt —
you sell polish and hope the client brings the adverts. Pipeline ownership is
how polish becomes a retainer."

**The pitch.** Studio: fifteen candidates, white-label documents, your firm's
name on everything the client opens. Your consultants keep client-facing
judgment; the machine reads 17 boards by default, 22 supported, every
morning before your kettle boils.

**The land-and-expand line.** "Start with your three most senior retainer
clients in Sovereign if counsel is involved — institutions pay for the
data sentence: everything stays on your servers."

## F · Universal rescue lines (when the room goes quiet)

- "What's the honest bottleneck — finding roles, or paperwork at speed?" (If they
  say neither, ask who they placed last month and let silence work.)
- "The demo is one candidate, free forever, on your own machine. If it embarrasses
  me I want to know by Friday."
- "Nothing sends without your tap. That's not a safety disclaimer, it's the product."
- "Check the letter against the CV. If it invented one fact, delete the zip and
  tell everyone. It can't — the drafting pass is forbidden from inventing, in code."
- "Twenty rand a word is what clients pay ghostwriters per tailored letter. The
  machine drafts nightly; you keep the per-letter fee."
